Galaxy Viewer Is Entering Its Final Stage

I’ve been unusually quiet for the past couple of weeks because I’ve been deep in the catalog. I have personally reviewed more than 10,000 astronomical images, one by one—discarding duplicates, less compelling views, science banners, charts, and images that simply do not belong in the experience. I’m happy to report that Galaxy Viewer has made major progress.

Galaxy Viewer now contains 1,814 curated destinations from major observatories, space missions, and astronomical archives. My goal is approximately 3,000 images before launch, and the database will continue growing after release.

Galaxy Viewer grew out of my fascination—and honestly my perplexity—with the sheer scale of the universe. I started by wondering how the stars and galaxies around us could be mapped in a way that actually feels connected. I realized I could not find an app that let me travel through the sky and connect some of its most beautiful locations with the extraordinary high-definition imagery produced by the world’s great observatories.

The biggest breakthrough was learning how to place the high-definition observatory image directly over the live Aladin sky engine. Galaxy Viewer decodes astronomical metadata and performs precise pixel-scale registration, orientation, field-of-view calibration, blending, vignetting and cross-fading so the HD image becomes part of the sky instead of feeling pasted on top of it. I also developed a custom star-field filtering effect that gives dense multi-star scenes a subtle glittering response as you travel into them.

At roughly 30 seconds per trip, visiting all 1,814 destinations once would take about 15.1 hours of continuous travel. A 3,000-image catalog would represent about 25 hours—before revisiting a single destination.

The first standalone Galaxy Viewer commit was uploaded to GitHub on July 20, 2026 at 21:15:46 UTC (4:15:46 PM in Colombia). That was 68 days ago. Since then I decided to give myself a serious education in coding and software development. After thousands of iterations, tests and fixes, I’m proud to say that software app developer is now another title I can add to my résumé.

I’m happy to announce that Galaxy Viewer is now in its final stages, and I’m targeting release on the Google Play Store within the next week or two. Download and image-loading performance have improved significantly, and this Friends & Family preview is frozen to this exact stable build so my ongoing development work will not interrupt your experience. Thank you for being part of the journey. Tap Launch and go explore the universe.

— Germán

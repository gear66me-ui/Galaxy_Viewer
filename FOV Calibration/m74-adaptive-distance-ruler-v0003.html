Adaptive Galaxy Distance Ruler Lab

v0003 · test branch only

Test whether a ruler can keep a meaningful angular and physical label while Aladin's field of view changes by many orders of magnitude.

Reference galaxy: M74 / NGC 628

Use a nearby, face-on spiral galaxy. NASA/ESA report a distance of about 32 million light-years. For this experiment, set the working diameter to 100,000 light-years (a round calibration target, not a precision measurement of its faint outer boundary). Its expected angular diameter under the small-angle approximation is about 10.75 arcmin.

Equations used

α (degrees) = Aladin.angularDist(x₁, y, x₂, y)

α (radians) = α (degrees) × π / 180

physical ruler (ly) ≈ distance (ly) × α (radians)

expected galaxy angular diameter ≈ diameter (ly) / distance (ly) × 180 / π

The physical-size equation is the small-angle approximation. For nearby M74 it is a useful first test. The chosen 100,000 ly diameter is a reference assumption; the angular ruler itself is the part being calibrated.
